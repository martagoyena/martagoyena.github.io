Pericardiocentesis XR Training

Exploring how different input devices can support interaction with an immersive simulation of a clinical procedure, with a focus on controllers, hand tracking and haptic gloves.

The challenge

Pericardiocentesis is a clinical procedure used to remove fluid from the space surrounding the heart. XR can provide a controlled environment in which learners can become familiar with procedural steps and practise interacting with virtual instruments.

Interaction is an important part of this experience. Controllers, tracked hands and haptic gloves offer different ways to manipulate virtual tools, and their suitability should be examined in the context of the task rather than assumed.

Research focus

  • Interaction with virtual medical instruments
  • Controllers, hand tracking and haptic gloves
  • Task execution and interaction behaviour
  • Usability, workload and user experience

Interaction methods

Controllers

Controller-based interaction provides a tracked input method for manipulating virtual instruments and carrying out the task.

Hand tracking

Hand tracking allows users to interact through their tracked hands, without relying on conventional handheld controllers.

Haptic gloves

SenseGlove Nova 2 gloves introduce a wearable input method with haptic feedback. Their setup and hand calibration are important parts of the interaction workflow.

My contribution

My work focuses on XR interaction and evaluation. This includes working with alternative input devices, considering their practical setup and calibration requirements, and examining how users interact with virtual instruments during the procedure.

The evaluation approach combines subjective measures with information about users' movements and interactions. The aim is to understand the experience of using each interaction method in the context of a specific medical-training task.

Evaluation dimensions

  • Usability — SUS
  • Perceived workload — NASA-TLX
  • Simulator sickness — SSQ / SSSQ, as applicable
  • Presence — IPQ
  • Movement and interaction data
  • Open-ended user feedback

Technical setup

The prototype is developed in Unity for an XR headset environment. The experimental setup includes an HTC XR Elite and SenseGlove Nova 2 gloves, alongside controller-based and hand-tracking interaction.

Device setup, calibration and tracking are relevant practical considerations when preparing an XR study. The evaluation also considers how interaction data can help characterise task execution.

Relevance to XR interaction research

This project connects spatial interaction, wearable haptics and human–computer interaction with a concrete training application. It provides a context for investigating how input devices and feedback shape the experience of manipulating virtual tools.
